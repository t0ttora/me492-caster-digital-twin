Python V2.3

I run routes and QR approaches in Python. The model logs what the controller requests, the torque at each drive wheel and how the robot moves.

The S-path run had 12.62 mm of cross-track RMSE. Both square runs finished in 32.50 s. The release passed 83 regression tests.

Each 0.50 m square completed four edges and four turns. In an earlier development sweep of 50 variants, 36 passed, 11 met part of the criteria and three failed. Those runs are still in the record.

Native Simscape

I ran the multibody model in MATLAB R2026b and recorded axle pose, caster motion, torque and contact loads. In eight runs I fed it the left and right axle torques from Python.

On its continuous-arc run, Simscape recorded 1.15 mm of cross-track RMSE. The Python and Simscape S-path controllers used different speed profiles.

The caster swivel rate remained above the convergence threshold at the smallest tested solver step of 6.25 µs. The direct 180° pivot also missed its 20 mm tolerance. In M09 you can play that run and the sequence with a short launch.

ROS 2 and Gazebo

On Ubuntu 24.04 arm64 I built four packages with ROS 2 Jazzy and Gazebo Harmonic. The live logs include the simulator clock, camera detections, wheel joints and odometry. They also record world pose and the commands that passed the guard.

The Linux build passed 37 regression tests. In M02, the robot entered the stop band 63 ms after the brake command.

In Q08, the controller read the wrong station tag 47 times, kept forward requests at zero and ended the search after 3.200 s of simulation time. In O05, it rejected 18 unauthorized requests while fresh odometry returned. Motion resumed after authorization and a new command.
